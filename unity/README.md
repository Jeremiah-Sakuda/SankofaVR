## Living gallery

The candidate scene is `Assets/Sankofa/Scenes/LivingGallery.unity`, package `Content/living-gallery.json` v0.4.0. One canonical framed work opens into the same scene around the stationary seat. One wide Enter control starts the authored default without inferring a preference; two smaller gallery controls explicitly select a gourd or remembered-meeting perspective, each with a short framed vignette; looking at or receiving an object does not choose a cultural or moral position. The contextual griot and question availability preserve the later reveal; the friendship question is offered after the agreement. Flour placement changes milk hospitality, with a receive or passive path on each arrangement.

The closing memento can be kept by the existing panel or by receiving it, holding an open palm over the labelled recess for 0.45 seconds and releasing it. Tracking loss cancels deposit arming. Contextual guidance and a brightening rim show the dwell/release sequence only when Keep is available; the object stays carried after a save failure for an explicit retry. New kept records preserve default/explicit perspective origin; older records retain unknown origin. A saved work offers the other thread directly, replay or the same water today. The existing illustrated shelf namespace and old clip keys preserve older plan/outcome replay; exact question/interruption replay remains unimplemented.

```sh
"/Applications/Unity/Hub/Editor/6000.3.25f1/Unity.app/Contents/MacOS/Unity" -batchmode -noaudio -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaSceneBuilder.BuildLivingGallery
"/Applications/Unity/Hub/Editor/6000.3.25f1/Unity.app/Contents/MacOS/Unity" -batchmode -noaudio -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaBuild.BuildLivingGallery
```

`artifacts/Sankofa-LivingGallery.apk` is a local review build. Generation and muted batch tests do not launch the XR Simulator. The frame image is generated from the actual historical scene; the modern background is draft generated art, with [prompt and provenance](Assets/Sankofa/Art/IllustratedTheatre/modern-river-v1.sources.json). No headset or cultural acceptance is implied. Audio files are bundled for product use; they were not played during this iteration at the user's request.

## Layout

```text
Assets/Sankofa/Core/         Story core: packages, plan composition and validation, story run, visit journal, kept visits (engine-free)
Assets/Sankofa/Flow/         Application flow: visit phases, choices, questions, keep/replay/continue/forget, cues, timing (engine-free)
Assets/Sankofa/Interaction/  Gesture logic: poke, open-palm receive, touch and head-direction attention (engine-free)
Assets/Sankofa/Storage/      Kept visits on the device: one JSON file, written atomically (engine-free)
Assets/Sankofa/Runtime/      Presentation and input: fire, narration, captions, panels, stage, transition, ambience, composition
Assets/Sankofa/Editor/       Project setup, scene generation, narration import, build, simulator launcher and build guards
Assets/Sankofa/Content/      The Enkare Nairobi story package, its narration manifest and the credits
Assets/Sankofa/Resources/Narration/  The griot's pre-rendered lines (MP3, imported as compressed mono)
Assets/Sankofa/Art/          Stereo-safe shaders; generated materials, meshes and noise; scanned materials;
                             field recordings (Recorded/, with sources.json); fonts
Assets/Sankofa/Scenes/       Hearth.unity, generated by SankofaSceneBuilder
Assets/Sankofa/Tests/        EditMode and PlayMode tests
Assets/Oculus, Assets/XR, Assets/Resources, Assets/Plugins/Android
                             Meta, OpenXR and manifest settings maintained by the tools
```

The engine-free folders are also compiled and tested outside Unity by [../dotnet](../dotnet). Unity enforces their dependency direction through assembly definitions: the core references nothing, the flow references only the core, and nothing engine-free references Unity.

`Hearth.unity` and the materials and meshes in `Art/Generated` are produced by code. Change `SankofaSceneBuilder`, `SankofaWaterScenes` or `SankofaArt` and regenerate rather than editing them by hand.

## Commands

The illustrated-theatre MVP is a separate scene and build. It reuses the first historical story and its continuation, with a separate local saved-visit shelf. Open **Sankofa > Build Illustrated Theatre MVP**, then Play, or use **Sankofa > Play Illustrated Theatre in XR Simulator**. **Sankofa > Build Illustrated Theatre APK** writes `artifacts/Sankofa-IllustratedTheatre.apk`. The baseline `Hearth` scene remains available for comparison.

Both APKs use `com.sankofa.quest`, so installing one replaces the other application build. Their saved-visit folders are separate.

Its content is `Content/illustrated-theatre-mvp.json`; spatial layouts, cast pose sheets and accepted/passive illustration responses are configured in `Content/illustrated-theatre-layout.json`. `IllustratedFigure` renders pose dissolves and the physical handover. `IllustratedTheatre` unfolds scenery and consumes resolved invitations. The core and input flow still decide every outcome; head-direction feedback cannot select a pose as an accepted action. Reduced motion reveals the scenic composition together, and pause freezes the reveal and pose clocks. Both generated atlases preserve alpha, use depth writes and ASTC compression on Android, and are drafts awaiting cultural/device review.

```sh
UNITY="/Applications/Unity/Hub/Editor/6000.3.25f1/Unity.app/Contents/MacOS/Unity"
"$UNITY" -batchmode -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaSceneBuilder.BuildIllustratedTheatre
"$UNITY" -batchmode -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaBuild.BuildIllustratedTheatre
SANKOFA_CAPTURE=1 "$UNITY" -batchmode -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -runTests -testPlatform PlayMode -testFilter Sankofa.Tests.IllustratedTheatreTests -testResults "$PWD/artifacts/logs/illustrated-playmode.xml"
sh scripts/make_illustrated_gallery.sh
```

The gallery command exports three review stills and a silent handover video from `artifacts/illustrated-theatre` into `docs/media`. Capture cadence is for inspection and does not measure headset performance.

From the repository root, with the editor installed through Unity Hub and a signed-in, licensed Unity account:

```sh
UNITY="/Applications/Unity/Hub/Editor/6000.3.25f1/Unity.app/Contents/MacOS/Unity"
"$UNITY" -batchmode -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaProjectSetup.Configure
"$UNITY" -batchmode -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaSceneBuilder.BuildScenes
"$UNITY" -batchmode -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -runTests -testPlatform EditMode -assemblyNames Sankofa.Tests.Editor -testResults "$PWD/artifacts/logs/editmode.xml"
"$UNITY" -batchmode -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaBuild.BuildMain
```

The same actions are in the editor's **Sankofa** menu. The APK is written to the ignored `artifacts/` folder. TextMeshPro's essential resources are committed under `Assets/TextMesh Pro` (without its emoji sprites). If they are ever missing, Unity's `-importPackage` option imports them synchronously; `AssetDatabase.ImportPackage` alone completes too late in batch mode:

```sh
"$UNITY" -batchmode -quit -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -importPackage "$PWD/unity/Library/PackageCache/$(ls unity/Library/PackageCache | grep com.unity.ugui@)/Package Resources/TMP Essential Resources.unitypackage"
```

Each build includes only its selected scene: `Hearth` for the baseline APK, or `IllustratedTheatre` for the MVP APK. The local competition brief and references live outside `Assets`.

Every player build, from any menu or script, fails if it would ship anything from `Assets/LocalOnly`, files in `Assets/StreamingAssets`, or stale run files that Unity's performance-test package can leave in `Assets/Resources` after batch test runs; the build command deletes those first. After the build, the APK is scanned to confirm Meta's development credentials were removed.

## Narration

The griot's lines are generated ahead of time from the package, so a build never calls a service to speak. With the gcloud CLI signed in to an account that can use Cloud Text-to-Speech on the Sankofa Google Cloud project, and `ffmpeg` installed:

```sh
python3 scripts/generate_narration.py unity/Assets/Sankofa/Content/enkare-nairobi.json
```

The script renders each line with Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS (voice Kore, the original griot direction). It writes `Resources/Narration/enkare-nairobi/<line>.mp3` and records each line's clip and length in the package. Unchanged lines are skipped by fingerprint. A line the provider refuses is reported, and the script exits non-zero. Rerun it to retry. Regenerate the scenes afterwards so the stage matches the package.

The illustrated story revision uses a separate clip namespace, `enkare-nairobi.illustrated-v2`, and shares unchanged baseline clips. For later revisions, choose a **new** namespace with `--clip-namespace` so kept plans retain the speech they reference; `--only` selects changed line IDs. Each new manifest records the provider, model, voice, direction, exact text and fingerprint. Failed or implausibly long renders are rejected and cannot silently reuse speech for different words. These checks do not establish pronunciation or listening quality. The offline pipeline tests are `python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts -p 'test_generate_narration.py'` from the repository root. The current script and response map are in [the story document](../docs/content/enkare-nairobi/stories.md#current-illustrated-episode-what-we-carry).

## Tests

The EditMode tests check that every component is wired, that the staged objects and scenes match the bundled package, that controls and resting places are within seated reach, that hands draw over the transition curtain, that the build guards work and that the engine-free core runs under Unity's runtime.

PlayMode tests run the real scene with its narration, muted, without a headset. The first visit goes before the railway, takes the gourd, asks a question, touches, keeps the visit, follows it to today, and replays it exactly. The second is a passive visit when the railway came. The third returns early from a story. Any Sankofa error fails the test. It drives the flow directly, so it is software integration evidence, not simulator or device evidence. With `SANKOFA_CAPTURE=1` the same suite also renders review frames from the seated eye into `artifacts/captures/`. The capture suite also writes a silent 22-second `performance-*.png` approach/exchange/return study at a fixed 12 fps after the final poses; `scripts/make_gallery.sh` encodes it for review. This cadence is not measured device performance. It also writes 30-second mono mixdowns of the ambience at the hearth and in each story scene, as heard from the seat (distance falloff, no spatialisation, no narration). These are desktop renders and a desktop mix, not Quest visuals or headset audio:

```sh
"$UNITY" -batchmode -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -runTests -testPlatform PlayMode -testResults "$PWD/artifacts/logs/playmode.xml"
SANKOFA_CAPTURE=1 "$UNITY" -batchmode -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -buildTarget Android -runTests -testPlatform PlayMode -testFilter Sankofa.Tests.HearthCaptures -testResults "$PWD/artifacts/logs/captures.xml"
```

## What the slice does

- **Start.** A title card over the fire names the experience and the place and says how to choose. The fire burns and the night sounds play, but nothing is spoken until **Begin** (or **Leave**).
- **Choosing.** Every button can be pressed with a fingertip, or from the seat by pointing and pinching. With Touch controllers, point and pull the trigger. A select lands when the pinch or trigger is released over the same button, so sweeping across buttons presses nothing. In the editor with no headset, the mouse points.
- **Welcome.** The fire speaks: "Come. Sit with me." It then asks when to go: *Before the railway* or *When the railway came*. The visitor can choose during the welcome. Without input the fire waits at the hearth.
- **Entry.** The fire rises into a curtain of warm light, and the stream where the forest meets the plain surrounds the stationary visitor. The camera never moves. The ember crosses into the scene as the traders' fire or the camp lantern.
- **The story.** People are fictional composites with generic CC0 faces and project-made clothing; the ewe is a shape of firelight. No real-person likeness is claimed. They walk in along paths over the ground when they appear, go where the story sends them (Wambui and Naserian kneel at the hide to trade; Njeri goes to Naisula's side), and walk away when they part. Each era has a hand moment: a gourd held out to an open palm (with a nearby button), and then touching the water, the trading hide or the rails. Lines after it differ by what the visitor did. Without input, a passive default plays, and the fire never says the visitor chose it.
- **Questions.** A panel by the right hand offers authored questions, including one about whatever the visitor has been looking at. An answer interrupts and then resumes the story from a safe boundary. Head direction is recorded only as a signal, never as consent or eye tracking.
- **Return.** The world folds back through the light, and the keepsake (a bead or a seed bundle) rests on the right hearth stone. **Keep this visit**, Begin again or Leave.
- **Kept visits.** Only an explicit Keep saves a visit, as one local file. The welcome back then offers the kept path and outcomes to replay (questions and interruption timing are not replayed), follow to the same water today, start a new story, or forget it, after a confirmation. Today recalls only what was kept.
- **Interruption.** Pause (button, headset removal or the system menu) stops speech and freezes every timer, and the figures, the ewe, the traffic and incidental sounds hold still. Resume restarts the line from its start without repeating its actions. **Return to the fire** leaves the story at any point and is never recorded as finishing it.
- **Settings.** Caption size, reduced motion (a short dim dissolve with no rising fire), ambience level (full, low or off), recenter, and in development builds the fire comparison below. In the illustrated theatre, reduced motion also uses a direct pose swap and a light acknowledgement without card turning.

Each cue is logged as `[Sankofa] <cue> at <visit time>`, and the visit journal is logged when the visitor reaches the closing. Read them with `adb logcat -s Unity` on a headset.

## How the hearth is made

The hearth's geometry and lighting are generated from code, with no AI-generated media; its sound is field recordings. Its surfaces use four scanned CC0 materials from Poly Haven (rock, burned ground, dry ground, bark; see the [asset register](../docs/asset-register.md)), projected triplanar on stones, from above on the ground (blended from dry earth to burned ground by a per-vertex burn amount around the fire), and along the grain on wood, with a large-scale procedural variation to hide tiling. The pieces: noise-displaced stones (sooted on their fire-facing sides) and pebbles, irregular logs with bark grain, a laid and leaning fire of five flame tongues on coals whose cracks glow and pulse with the flames, seats and twigs, terrain with an ash ring and distant hills, grass that sways, tree silhouettes, and a night sky with stars and a moon. Sparks burst in time with the loud cracks found in the recorded fire; thin smoke and a halo stand in for volumetrics and bloom.

Lighting is two-toned: the warm fire, whose centre wanders a few centimetres, and faint cool moonlight with rim light and low mist over the distance. Soft fire shadows, moon shadows and ambient occlusion are ray-traced once by `SankofaBake` into vertices, because neither the fire nor the moon moves relative to the hearth. Every shader ends with a shared grade (`SankofaGrade.cginc`) in place of post-processing: a filmic tone curve so the flame rolls off rather than clipping, slight desaturation, a multiplicative split tone, and dithering against banding.

Static pieces are combined per material. The current scene costs are recorded in the decision log and verified by EditMode checks; these geometry proxies do not establish actual Quest performance. Canopies use small three-dimensional sprig clusters with two-sided cutout leaves, depth writes, restrained wind and coverage-preserving texture mipmaps. Rectangular leaf cards are excluded from the shadow bake so they cannot cast solid rectangular shadows. Alpha overdraw still needs device profiling.

## How the story scenes are made

The three scenes in [the scene scope](../docs/content/enkare-nairobi/scenes.md) are generated the same way, in `SankofaWaterScenes`.

- **Shared ground.** A stream crosses about 2.4 m ahead, in a narrow channel whose banks hide the water's edges. The forest rises on the left and the plain falls away on the right.
- **Before the railway.** A spring spills over a rock within reach. The traders' fire lights the scene, with a hide on a flat rock between the visitor and the stream.
- **When the railway came.** The bank is trampled and cut. Bell tents and timber stand across the stream, an embankment runs beyond, and a crib of sleepers and rails is within reach.
- **Today.** The river runs between a raised garden bed, a stall with a charcoal *jiko*, a painted gate, streetlights and a city whose windows come from world position in the City shader.

People use a skinned CC0 base character and hair from Quaternius's *Universal Base Characters*, with compatible motion from the *Universal Animation Library* (`Art/Figures`). Project-made fitted tops and folded wraps share the skeleton. Collar and sleeve weights follow the source joints; covered body faces are removed after garment derivation to prevent skin poking through. `EmberFigure.shader` lights matte bronze skin and quiet woven cloth from the scene's fire/lantern, ambient and moon; a restrained ember outline connects them to the fire. A depth pre-pass prevents layered surfaces doubling their light. Generic faces, short hair and clothing are theatrical drafts awaiting family/context review. Family lines retain distinct warm garment colours.

`FigureMotion` walks a figure along paths baked from the terrain and drives its animator: walk, idle, quiet kneeling rest, a reach to give, and talking gestures. Walking pace uses editor-measured clip stance speed, with route acceleration and final braking. `FigureGrounding` corrects stance feet in the stage frame over authored route heights; it does not sense physical terrain. What a figure carries follows her hand or neck bone. The story moves a figure with a `move` action to an object's approach point; that point says whether one stands or kneels there. The ewe is still a procedural shape posed by the shader.

Water uses an opaque `Water.shader`: there is no transparency cost, and it reflects the sky and glints in the scene's warm light and moonlight. Each scene has its own ambience of field recordings (`StoryAmbience`; see the [asset register](../docs/asset-register.md)): a brook, Maasai Mara crickets and birds, wind in grass, a distant animal bell, the traders' small fire, canvas, and a matatu stage and far city today. The ewe bleats and hammering sounds along the line now and then (`ObjectSound`). No recorded voices are placed in the past, since the people whose languages they would be have not been asked. It ducks under the narrator like the night at the hearth.

The people gesture through their animator and two-bone arm posing. `FigureExchange` resolves both actors after base animation and before finger poses: attention moves from partner to vessel and back, with a small lean and acknowledgment. Explicit visitor/story glances have priority. The receiver supports her vessel and the giver's wrist follows its tilt. Hand anchors follow the skeleton, gifts remain offered until taken, and the authored sharing scene settles both gourds, tilts the giver's, pours between their actual openings and releases. `FigurePresence` adds staggered blinks and held-object finger poses, returning the hand to rest when its object leaves. These gestures do not advance story state and use the paused stage clock. The ewe retains its procedural gait and shader posing. Stage-relative targets survive recentering and height changes; pausing freezes the exchange clock.

Captions and control labels use TextMeshPro's signed-distance-field type in Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for low-vision readability (LiberationSans is the fallback for missing glyphs), which stays crisp at any distance in a headset. Captions sit on a soft dark backing fitted to the line, with a thin outline for contrast against flame and sky; a medium caption line subtends at least one degree from the seat, which the PlayMode test checks. The control panels are firelit wooden plaques with warm labels of one consistent size.

The night is recorded: crickets from the Maasai Mara placed around the clearing, the Mara's night further off, and wind in dry grass, each looping from a different point. The ambience ducks to about half while the narrator speaks and follows the visitor's ambience setting.

Every sound except the voice comes from Freesound field recordings under CC0 or CC BY. `scripts/prepare_recordings.py` edits them from the raw downloads in the ignored `vendor/freesound/` folder: trimming, folding to mono, setting levels and crossfading seamless loops. Nothing is synthesised. **Credits** in the settings shows the required attributions, and an EditMode test fails if any source plays a clip that is not a listed recording.

## Fire presence comparison

The stress test's T3 asks whether a directed fire feels more responsive than a simple one. In development builds the settings panel's **Fire** button switches between the directed fire (state cues, lean while listening, breathing with speech) and a steady fire with the same narration. Use it only with recorded narration and counterbalanced order. Caption-only lines drive the fire from a synthetic speech rhythm, which is not a fair comparison.

## Running in the Meta XR Simulator

Without a headset, the hearth runs in Play mode against the [Meta XR Simulator](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/xrsim-intro/), installed at `/Applications/MetaXRSimulator.app`. Use **Sankofa > Play Hearth in XR Simulator**, or with the editor closed:

```sh
"$UNITY" -projectPath "$PWD/unity" -executeMethod Sankofa.EditorTools.SankofaSimulator.PlayHearth
```

The simulator starts with controllers: aim with the mouse and click to pull the trigger at a panel. Switch the simulated hands to hand input to try the open palm, touch and pinch. The simulator exercises flow, visuals and wiring; it is not evidence of hand-tracking reliability, comfort, sound or Quest performance.

## Running on a headset

Installing a development build needs a Quest in developer mode, which needs a Meta developer account set up by the owner. Unity's Android support includes `adb` at `/Applications/Unity/Hub/Editor/6000.3.25f1/PlaybackEngines/AndroidPlayer/SDK/platform-tools/adb` if it is not on your path.

```sh
adb install -r artifacts/Sankofa.apk
```

Sit down before launching: the hearth is placed in front of where your head is when tracking settles, and **Recenter** places it again.

## Implemented but unverified

Unit, EditMode, PlayMode and build checks establish software behaviour only. Still unknown until device and participant evidence exists: whether the palm, touch and question gestures work reliably with real hand tracking; the narration's pronunciation and pacing on a headset; the figures' legibility and comfort at close range; panel, offer and keepsake reach and comfort for different bodies; the fire's look and frame cost; comfort of the curtain transition; caption legibility; the recorded sound mix; and spatial voice placement (Unity's built-in panning, not an HRTF spatializer).

Known review items before release:

- Meta's setup tool adds a telemetry project identifier to the Android manifest and lists every Quest model as supported. Disclose or remove the identifier as the privacy notice requires, and list only devices with recorded evidence.
- Unity's performance-test package (a dependency of `com.unity.collections`) writes build metadata into every player build's Resources by design: Unity version, player settings and the package list, with no paths, names or credentials (checked in the October 1 APK). Decide whether to strip it from release builds.
