## Editorial decision

Keep the existing dramatic openings and endings. The gourd conflict is particularly strong: one woman protects food at home, and the other accepts a cost at her own home. The visitor follows and witnesses; neither woman needs an outsider to make the agreement possible.

Fix the short passages and questions that sit between those moments. Make factual context concise and useful. Keep the full citation and its limitations available in source details. Imagined status stays visible. Any words spoken as documented context still need exact approved text and source checks; a warmer voice does not relax that contract.

## Six sides, six different reasons to care

Use this matrix to review endings and staging. It is an editorial test, not new narration to insert everywhere.

| Side | What the visitor initially sees | What following this side adds | A useful reason to revisit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Journey: waiting | A woman may leave before dark | Waiting costs her daylight; she nearly left | What kept the other woman coming? |
| Journey: late | The empty path | Continuing is a choice made without knowing whether anyone remains | What did waiting cost the woman already there? |
| Gourd: brought less | A shortfall in a promised load | The portion left at home gives the shortfall meaning | What had the other household counted on? |
| Gourd: expected more | Someone ready to receive the flour | Accepting less changes her own meal | What did the first woman refuse to take from home? |
| Water: remembers | A remembered place may be wrong | Honesty makes sharing the memory possible | How does the younger listener carry the memory onward? |
| Water: traveller | A disagreement about reeds | Asking about sound becomes a different way to inherit memory | What does admitting uncertainty cost the person remembering? |

Do not make the choice a moral quiz or mark one perspective correct. The two sides need different understanding, not contradictory outcomes. The stage turn should put attention on the person whose new information is being heard.

## Source and cultural checks

- The Akan product name, West African griot inspiration and Kenyan setting are separate elements. “Stories from many fires” frames the host; it does not establish a Kenyan storytelling tradition or cultural approval.
- Review the characters' dress and voice with someone relevant to the represented setting; use the specific comments to make targeted corrections. A generic authenticity score is not useful.
- Do not remove an unfamiliar cultural detail solely to broaden appeal. Explain it when it affects the story. Conversely, do not add a Maa/Gĩkũyũ word simply as decoration without meaning and pronunciation review.
- These people and exchanges are authored fiction. “Histories we did not get to hear” is the mission; “we recovered this family's lost account” would be false for this content.
- Preserve limits on C1/C6/C7. Neither a present-day museum object nor a broad historical trade relationship proves a particular 1850s household, garment, vessel or bargain.
