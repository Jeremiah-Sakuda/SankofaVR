# Enkare Nairobi: the stories

October 4, 2026 · Draft 1 for family and cultural review · Narration is a working draft for pacing and voice. Bracketed IDs such as [C4] refer to the [source register](sources.md). A line without an ID is reconstruction or the griot's own voice. **Gated** lines wait for a medium-confidence claim to be confirmed. Character names are working names for the family to replace.

## The shape of it

One place, many times. The visitor sits at the griot's fire and chooses *when* to go:

- **Before the railway (mid-1800s).** Two women traders meet at the cool-water stream.
- **When the railway came (1899).** Their granddaughters meet at the same water, in a famine year, as iron arrives.
- **The same water today.** This route unlocks only with a keepsake from an earlier visit.

The visit always begins and ends at the hearth. The ember the fire gives you becomes the light of each era:
- a traders' fire among three stones;
- a camp lantern in 1899;
- the coal of a *jiko*, a charcoal stove, today.

**Dramatic spine:** trust that crosses a boundary. Each era asks whether the old agreement between the forest and the plain still holds when there isn't enough. The answer turns into kinship and outlasts the land itself. This is the user's own two lineages: Kikuyu and Maasai.

**The griot.** This is the user's original Sankofa griot, unhurried, warm and a little wry. It tells the visitor plainly what is true and what is imagined.

**Gestures.** Every hand moment has a gesture, a nearby select alternative, and a passive path that never claims the visitor chose it.

| Moment | Line | Fire |
|---|---|---|

| Welcome 2 | I am a griot, a keeper of the stories that time tries to scatter. *(original)* | telling |
| Welcome 3 | Tonight, I reach back for you. *(original)* | telling |
| Welcome 4 | There is a stream where the forest meets the plain, so cool that the people who watered their herds there named the place for it. [C1] I have been back to it many times. | telling |

| Choices | **Before the railway** · **When the railway came** · and after a kept visit, **The same water, today** | |
| Waiting aside (once, after a long pause) | No hurry. A fire is patient; that is most of what it is good for. | listening |
| Honesty (asked by hand: "Is this true?") | The place is true. The trading was true, and so were the hard years. The people I will show you are made of many people. That is how I tell it. | holding |
| Greeting | *To be chosen by the family:* a Gĩkũyũ and a Maa greeting the griot offers first. | welcoming |

Every story then begins with the same ember: "Every story I tell begins with a little of my light. Hold out your open hand." The visitor **receives the ember**, or the fire sets it beside them.

**Hand questions:**
- *What is banana flour for?* Answered from C4.
- *Why does a woman come alone?* **Gated on C5:** "Because the women carried the trade, and the old agreements said a trader was not to be touched, even when the young men were raiding."
- *Are they friends?* "Trading partners first. Then, often, family." [C6]
- *Is this a real place?* "Yes. A city stands on it now and carries its name." [C1, C3]

**Noticing (head direction):** the water, the ewe, the gourd and the forest edge each surface a question. Nothing in the story advances because of a glance.

**Two-path differences:** whether you carried and set down the flour changes who Naserian speaks to, how the bead reaches you, and the recall line in the "today" continuation.

**Dramatic question:** the two families lived by trading at this water. Now there is nothing to trade, and strangers are building on the meeting place. What do the granddaughters keep?

**Hand questions:**
- *Who are the workers?* "Men brought from India to build the line, a very long way from their own homes. Many never went back. Listen: you can hear them working." [C3, C16]
- *Why is it called the famine of Ruraya?* **Gated** on the family confirming the name's meaning.
- *What happened to the cattle?* From C9, without figures.
- *Did they keep meeting?* "I don't know. I hope so. That is the honest answer."

**Handling:** hunger is told through absence: no bells, empty gourds, quiet. No suffering bodies, no mortality numbers, and the railway is neither a villain nor a hero.

## What stays out of the MVP

- **The visitor's own fragments**, a family word or a place, woven into the framing by a live model. This would be bounded, labelled as the visitor's own account, and never infer ancestry.
- **More eras** at this water, for example the 1920s town or Independence in 1963. Each needs its own review.
- **Character voices beyond the griot.** The griot voices everyone, as a storyteller would.
- **Music.** Only licensed recordings of real performers; otherwise natural sound and silence.
