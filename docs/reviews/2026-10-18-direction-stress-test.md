# Sankofa direction stress test

October 18, 2026. Adversarial review of the [experience transformation proposal](../product/experience-transformation-plan.md), including its assumptions and experiments.

**The illustrated theatre remains a credible format. Object-triggered memory has not earned its position as the leading product bet.** Prove that a complete encounter interests people, then establish what spatial staging contributes, then decide whether an additional action improves it. The existing story also remains a hypothesis; preserving it is not a condition of preserving Sankofa.

These are proposed corrections, not an approved scope replacement. The [PRD](../product/prd.md) still governs, including its unresolved personalisation requirements. No runtime work or participant testing was performed for this review.

## The strongest case against the proposal

The finished experience could be an attractive, conscientious illustration of a familiar lesson: one person accepts a shortfall because the other previously showed patience. Visitors hold props while narration explains the meaning. They understand the plot, complete every gesture and distinguish fiction from fact, yet feel little anticipation or desire to recommend it.

That product could pass much of the proposed acceptance matrix. **Comprehension, interaction reliability and ethical clarity are necessary; together they do not establish entertainment value.** A memory effect could make a weak scene more elaborate without making it more involving.

The opposite error is also possible: rejecting a quiet, embodied work because its written synopsis lacks a large twist. Modest stakes, hospitality and silence can carry a strong experience. A text exercise can reveal causal confusion; it cannot settle whether a performed spatial encounter is affecting.

## Assumptions that fail or remain open

| Assumption in the proposal | Stress-test finding | Proposed correction |
|---|---|---|
| Handling the gourd creates meaningful discovery | Current memories provide both the relevant fact and its interpretation. A gesture may only start exposition | Ask what the visitor believed before the moment, what they noticed, and whether their interpretation changed. Accurate recall alone does not establish discovery |
| Both motives must be explained before the agreement | Both present needs need to be intelligible. Revealing the entire earlier exchange first may make the outcome predictable | Test the prior waiting as a reappraisal at or after Naserian's decision, rather than defining its earlier placement as essential |
| The 90-second comparison proves the emotional benefit | It ends before hospitality, which the plan calls the emotional peak | Use an excerpt to diagnose input. Judge its value again in the complete encounter and ending |
| Comparing two spatial versions establishes VR's contribution | Their difference is input, not spatial presentation | Separate the contribution of the story, spatial staging and new input; do not attribute all improvement to the mechanic |
| Four of six is a meaningful adoption rule | Different subsets could pass action, recall and preference; second exposure teaches the story; novelty and coaching can drive preference | Keep participant-level observations, first-exposure evidence and reasons. Treat counts as descriptions, not a winning threshold |
| A directed short is automatically the right fallback | It reduces implementation risk but may weaken differentiation and meaningful personalisation | Treat it as the production control. It must earn interest, and any narrowed product promise must be explicit |
| A good standalone short answers the competition brief sufficiently | Repeat use and rewarding purpose appear in the main judging guidance | Acknowledge this competitive weakness. Demonstrate a worthwhile second encounter if one exists; do not claim a habit from Keep or replay |
| A verified historical detail restores the original mission | Accurate texture does not make an invented meeting a recovered account, nor necessarily make the setting matter dramatically | Make regional context influence the encounter and obtain substantive cultural input; describe historical imagination honestly |

## A stronger dramatic hypothesis using existing material

The most promising thread in the actual script is **showing up when you cannot keep your promise**. It is already present in three lines: Wambui nearly turned back; she did not want Naserian waiting with no word; she promises to come herself next time. The gourd contains an inadequate offering that she nevertheless chose to bring.

Naserian's household need currently sits in an optional answer. The main story says she walked there counting on the flour. Bring enough of her present need into the encounter that accepting less visibly costs her something. Do not invent imminent starvation, a ruined household or a broken friendship to manufacture larger stakes; the current text establishes none of those.

A rough alternative sequence:

1. Enter while the exchange is unresolved. Establish what was promised and why both women need it.
2. Wambui asks the guest to hold what she brought. Her limited portions explain why coming was difficult.
3. The guest may help set it down. Their help cannot make the amount sufficient or decide the bargain.
4. Naserian permits the sheep to leave. The earlier waiting then becomes legible through her line, a brief spatial echo, or both.
5. Let hospitality show that the encounter has made room for the visitor. Leave the balance unpaid and the promise to return alive; avoid explaining the entire moral afterward.

This is a hypothesis about sequencing, not a demand for a twist. It fails if the decision feels arbitrary, the explanation feels artificially withheld, or visitors find the earlier, fully explained version more involving. It also fails if suspense disrupts the unhurried welcome that people actually value.

The characters need personality beyond their positions in a lesson: a particular way of speaking, competence, pride, humor or a remembered irritation. These should come from considered writing and cultural collaboration, not generic embellishment. The visitor's role remains a guest and optional helper. That is a legitimate theatrical role; it is narrower than controlling history or receiving a story generated about oneself.

The relevant written evidence is in [the current package](../../unity/Assets/Sankofa/Content/living-gallery.json): `e1.wambui`, `e1.naserian`, `q.gallery.household`, `e1.portions`, `e1.turn-intro`, `gallery.remember.answer`, `e1.turn` and `e1.after`.

## What Sankofa must retain

The [original web product](../product/adaptation.md) gave visitors a reason to explore through their own interests and fragments. The Quest candidate offers a fictional encounter chosen by its author. That can be an excellent adaptation, but personal relevance has changed from an input to a hoped-for response.

We should preserve the mission of making overlooked lives approachable without advertising invented people as recovered testimony. The existing [source register](../content/enkare-nairobi/sources.md) separates regional context from the invented meeting, prior waiting, hospitality and gifts. A claim label cannot supply the texture, authorship or human connection that the experience still needs.

A useful editorial question is: if we changed the names, replaced the gourds with mugs and moved the scene elsewhere, what meaningful understanding would disappear? A universal emotional theme is an advantage. A setting that supplies only labels and costume is a weakness. The answer may lie in the connected livelihoods, journeys, material objects and gathering place already present; those details need review and dramatic purpose, not more spoken citations.

Receiving an object can produce social acknowledgment without changing a plot. That should be described as responsive participation. It does not by itself fulfill the PRD's broader requirement that contrasting interests change multiple observable aspects of the telling. Preserve useful authored differences while testing, and explicitly reconcile scope before calling a single directed story the completed personalised product. Adding an AI service merely to restore a checklist would not solve this product question.

The fire and painting are strong framing devices. Neither is untouchable if visitors find it distracting, confusing or emotionally distant. The same applies to the bead: it must earn its farewell role, not merely provide a save token. Cultural review may change material, voice or narrative choices before final production.

## Competitive tradeoffs

The official rubric gives equal weight to innovation, experience, technical implementation and polish. Its main criteria also mention repeat use and habit-forming purpose. A new gesture can help differentiation, but cannot compensate for a flat story, unreliable hands or weak performance. A polished short may improve three areas while leaving repeat value limited. That is a real tradeoff, not a reason to invent daily rewards. [Official judging guidance](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/judging), checked October 18.

The stronger claim that the concept would fall apart on another platform belongs to the Boldest Original Concept special award. It should not become a blanket prohibition on a directed Entertainment experience. [Special awards](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/special-awards).

Two possible internal winners deserve comparison:

- **A directed encounter:** the women, gourd, brief memory, hospitality and return form one well-performed scene. Advantage: attention and production effort stay on performance. Risk: familiar format, limited repeat value and reduced personalisation.
- **An encounter with meaningful inspection:** the same dramatic foundation allows a deliberate action to reveal something worth noticing. Advantage: curiosity becomes embodied participation. Risk: a scene-selection button disguised as an object, divided attention, and additional recovery work.

If neither makes the relationship interesting, test a different premise before more production: **find the water beneath the familiar city**. Existing modern-river and earlier gathering-place material could support a short journey from present-day sound to the water behind Nairobi's name, then back to continuing river care. It could strengthen the overlooked-place promise while reducing dependence on character performance. It could also become a thin fact tour or a misleading idyllic-past/polluted-present contrast. Test a synopsis or rough storyboard first; this is not authorization for a new multi-era build.

No reviewed precedent or panel supplies a defensible probability of winning. The current recommendation is about the next best use of effort, not certainty about the field.

## Replace the original experiment with a sequence of decisions

**First, test the encounter through its ending.** Make a rough complete cut with existing writing, images and recordings available for later authorized listening. Compare the current explanatory order with one alternative on paper before producing both. A few unfamiliar target viewers can expose repeated confusion and lack of a specific reason to continue. Ask “What happened?”, “What were you waiting to find out?” and “What, if anything, would you tell someone else about this?” Do not require the author's moral or character names in the answer. A flat presentation cannot reject spatial emotional value on its own.

**Second, isolate spatial contribution.** On accessible Quest hardware, keep the accepted content and pacing as constant as practical while comparing a simple panel treatment with embodied staging. This is a rough production decision, not a claim that a panel is the best possible non-VR adaptation. Look for concrete effects: attention to the right action, being addressed at believable scale, continuity of the object through a scene change, or distraction from having to inspect and listen simultaneously. Headset-versus-screen preference alone mixes too many differences to identify the cause.

**Third, test new input only if there is an unmet role for it.** Compare automatic staging with deliberate initiation, holding the scene otherwise constant. Existing receive/select input should be tried before creating a new gesture. Measure missed intent, coaching, interruptions and whether the action strengthens the complete ending. If it only makes the interface more entertaining while weakening the story, it has not passed.

Use first exposure for story interest; later exposure can diagnose affordances and recovery. Counterbalancing helps reveal order effects but does not restore surprise. Record outcomes per person so a comprehension failure is not hidden by somebody else's gesture preference. Start with a few observations and extend only to resolve a consequential disagreement, rather than committing in advance to seventeen separate novice sessions. Cultural reviewers and technical coverage are separate needs.

Choose the simpler form when benefits are mixed, unexplained or limited to novelty. Repeated causal confusion warrants one script revision. Repeated indifference after a minimally credible performance warrants reconsidering the encounter. Missing hardware or participants means the corresponding question is unanswered, not passed or disproved. End any uncomfortable session and address the cause.

For return value, offer an available, specific companion perspective without requiring a save, then observe an unpressured choice to continue or finish. This measures immediate interest only. A later return can be interpreted only when people actually have comparable access to the headset and build. Remove the proposed 3/8-in-72-hours pass rule; neither access failure nor a reminder-driven revisit establishes demand. Revealing both existing memories completely in the first visit would also consume much of the current alternate's new material. A shorter later telling is not automatically a new perspective; decide what remains worth discovering before promising it.

## Correct the capacity claim

The prior proposal estimates 94–137 combined hours for its enhanced path, including reserve. Keeping only T1, T2, T5, T7 and T8 already totals **55–80 hours**, or **61–88 with the stated reserve**. That excludes the reduced 4–6-hour experiment and any additional integration of a directed cut. The claimed 40–60-hour fallback is therefore not supported by its own estimates.

A 4–6-hour cap is reasonable for an initial decision exercise using existing material, including preparation, observation and analysis. It is a spending limit, not a guarantee that a polished comparison, recruitment and hardware access fit. Stop at the limit and report what remains unresolved. Do not conceal unfinished work in a subsequent polishing ticket.

Before allocating the remaining month, identify actual weekly capacity, device access, reviewer availability and the cost of required baseline fixes. Re-estimate the selected cut with explicit acceptance and delivery time. If only 40–60 total hours exist, something substantive must be removed or the intended acceptance remains incomplete; calling the same plan “lean” does not resolve the mismatch.

The immediate priority is the known baseline defects plus a bounded complete-story proof, with device access investigated in parallel. No novel gesture, pavilion expansion or final media production should be justified by the previous scoring thresholds alone.

## Review method and limits

Three fresh-context agents examined strategy, dramatic writing, and experiment/delivery design. They received the proposal and the mandatory repository context, not the previous panel conclusions or conversation. Their task deliberately sought counterarguments; they are not neutral human judges or audience evidence. The lead independently checked the story, proposal arithmetic, PRD, source register and current official judging pages.

The strongest disagreement with the previous proposal is the elevation of object-led memory before proof of the encounter. The strongest agreement is preserving the illustrated foundation, character autonomy and focused production. This review neither establishes that the directed form is best nor that the present script should be discarded.

No sound played, simulator launched, provider called or runtime file changed. Existing uncommitted application work was preserved. The original transformation proposal remains available so these corrections are visible rather than silently substituted for an approved product decision.
